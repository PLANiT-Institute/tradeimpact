# Validation Report — TI Framework Engine

**Build brief milestone M5.** The worked examples were removed in Automotive Technical
Guideline v1.8, and the data workbook's vehicle parameters are all "TO COLLECT". As confirmed
with the project owner (NOTES.md D4), validation is therefore an **engine-vs-independent-hand-
calculation** check over a committed reference fixture built from *illustrative* parameter
values. The ±1% acceptance test proves the engine's arithmetic is correct; it does **not**
assert the real-world accuracy of the (illustrative) inputs.

- Reference fixture: [`fixtures/reference_case.json`](fixtures/reference_case.json)
- Independent expected values: generated by a standalone raw-formula calculation (no engine
  imports), embedded in the fixture's `expected` block and re-asserted in
  [`tests/test_validation.py`](tests/test_validation.py).
- Reproduce: `ti validate --fixture fixtures/reference_case.json`

## 2. Layer-by-layer trace — Korea, scenario S2

**Layer 1** (Method B): `E_ref,KR(t) = 0.165 × (1 − 0.04344)^t × 13 000`.
**Layer 2 BEV**: `E = 0.18 × [0.41551 × (1 − 0.05)^t] × 13 000`.
**Layer 2 ICE**: `E = 0.160 × 13 000 = 2 080` (constant).
**Layer 3 gap**: `TI_gap(t) = E_ref(t) − E_prod(t)`.

| t | E_ref [kg/yr] | E_BEV [kg/yr] | gap_BEV | E_ICE [kg/yr] | gap_ICE |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0 | 2 145.0 | 972.3 | +1 172.7 | 2 080.0 | +65.0 |
| 1 | 2 051.8 | 923.7 | +1 128.1 | 2 080.0 | −28.2 |
| 2 | 1 962.7 | 877.5 | +1 085.2 | 2 080.0 | −117.3 |
| 5 | 1 717.9 | 752.3 | +965.5 | 2 080.0 | −362.1 |
| 10 | 1 375.8 | 582.1 | +793.6 | 2 080.0 | −704.2 |
| 14 | 1 151.8 | 474.2 | +677.7 | 2 080.0 | −928.2 |

**Per-vehicle cumulative TI** (Σ t=0..14):
- BEV: **+13 577.3 kgCO₂e** — a sustained contribution; the grid (5.0 %/yr) decarbonises
  faster than the fleet benchmark (4.34 %/yr), so the gap stays wide.
- ICE: **−7 185.9 kgCO₂e** — starts a marginal contribution (+65 at t=0) and turns into a
  growing lock-in liability by year 1, the textbook carbon-lock-in signal.

**Crossover (closed form), KR S2:**
- ICE: `t* = ln(2080 / 2145) / ln(1 − 0.04344) = 0.693 yr` — crosses into liability almost
  immediately (new ICE only marginally below the fleet average at sale).
- BEV: `t* = ln((0.18×0.41551) / 0.165) / ln((1−0.04344)/(1−0.05)) = −115.0 yr`.
  The equality lies before the sale year; because the grid decarbonises faster than the
  fleet benchmark, there is no future crossover and the BEV stays below the benchmark.

## 3. Firm-level result and acceptance

`TI_vehicle × V_c,v`, summed across placements, ÷1000 → tCO₂e.

| Scenario | Expected (independent) | Engine | Rel. error |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| S1 (STEPS) | 1 077 486.454 | 1 077 486.454 | 0.000% |
| S2 (NDC)   |   788 686.618 |   788 686.618 | 0.000% |
| S3 (NZE)   |   375 478.129 |   375 478.129 | 0.000% |

All within the ±1% tolerance (in fact exact to floating point). The **decomposition identity**
`TI_cohort = Σ_c TI_country,c = Σ_v TI_powertrain,v` holds for every scenario (asserted in
`test_validation.py::test_decomposition_identity_all_scenarios`).

**Scenario monotonicity** confirms the intended economics: a clean-vehicle-heavy portfolio
scores *highest* under S1 (slow benchmark) and *lowest* under S3 (ambitious benchmark), because
a faster-declining benchmark shrinks every BEV's contribution while deepening every ICE's
liability.

## 4. Method B vs Method C cross-check

`bc_divergence(MethodB, MethodC, T)` flags any year where the two Layer-1 methods diverge by
more than 30% (Guideline §2.3, §6.1). The check is unit-tested in
`tests/test_layer1.py::test_bc_divergence_flag` (a fast-renewing two-bin fleet correctly trips
the >30% flag) and `::test_bc_divergence_identical_is_zero`.
