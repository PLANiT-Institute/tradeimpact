==Background==

In May 2018 KEPCO announced that it was in talks with a "local partner" and was investing US$2 billion to develop a 1,000-MW coal-fired plant in Sual but did not name the partner.<ref>[http://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/655206/kepco-in-talks-with-local-partner-to-develop-1-000-mw-sual-power-plant/story/ KEPCO in talks with local partner to develop 1,000-MW Sual power plant], GMA News Online, May 31, 2018</ref> In June 2018, in what appears to be a reference to the same project, SK Engineering & Construction (SKE&C), a subsidiary of South Korean conglomerate SK Group, announced that it had signed a Letter of Intent (LOI) with the Government of the Philippines to build two 600 MW coal-fired power plants on Luzon island at an estimated cost of KRW2.2 trillion (US$1.98 billion).<ref>[http://www.koreaittimes.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=83851 SK Engineering & Construction signs a 2-billion-dollarcontract to build coal-fired plants in the Philippines], Korea Times, Jun. 18, 2018</ref> 

In January 2019 KEPCO signed a memorandum of understanding with Trans-Asia to acquire the site of Trans-Asia's proposed 3 x 300 MW [[Baquioen power station]] and use it for KEPCO's 1 x 1,000 MW Sual KEPCO power station.<ref>[http://manilastandard.net/business/power-technology/286666/kepco-plans-to-build-1-000-mw-coal-fired-plant-in-pangasinan.html Kepco plans to build 1,000-MW coal-fired plant in Pangasinan], Manila Standard, Jan. 31, 2019</ref> In August 2019 the Philippine Department of Energy (DOE) listed the plant as a 2 x 500 MW project with a commissioning date of 2024.<ref>[https://www.doe.gov.ph/sites/default/files/pdf/electric_power/luzon_indicative_2019_jan_aug.pdf PRIVATE SECTOR INITIATED POWER PROJECTS (LUZON) INDICATIVE], Philippine Department of Energy, 31 August 2019</ref> The project will cost an estimated US$1.834 billion or P91.7 billion.<ref>[https://businessmirror.com.ph/2019/02/01/kephilco-keen-on-pursuing-coal-plant-project-in-sual-pangasinan/ Kephilco keen on pursuing coal plant project in Sual, Pangasinan], Business Mirror, Feb. 1, 2019</ref>

In October 2020 KEPCO announced that it would no longer finance foreign coal-fired power plants with the exception of [https://www.gem.wiki/Banten_Suralaya_power_station Jawa-9 and Jawa-10] in Indonesia and [https://www.gem.wiki/Banten_Suralaya_power_station Vung Ang-2 power station] in Vietnam. “We hope that this is not a false promise and that KEPCO will soon make an explicit statement that it is withdrawing from the Sual 2 project,” said Ian Rivera of the Philippine Movement for Climate Justice (PMCJ).<ref>[https://news.mongabay.com/2020/10/south-koreas-move-away-from-coal-leaves-a-philippine-power-plant-in-limbo/ South Korea’s move away from coal leaves a Philippine power plant in limbo], Mongabay, Oct. 26, 2020</ref>

In October 2020, KEPCO announced that it was cancelling its investment in this project.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.power-eng.com/2020/10/20/south-koreas-kepco-cancels-foreign-coal-power-investment/#gref|title=South Korea’s KEPCO cancels foreign coal power investment|last=|first=|date=October 20, 2020|website=Power Engineering|url-status=live|archive-url=|archive-date=|access-date=}}</ref> According to the Philippine Movement for Climate Justice (PMCJ) the project is cancelled.<ref>Communication with PMCJ, January 2021</ref>
