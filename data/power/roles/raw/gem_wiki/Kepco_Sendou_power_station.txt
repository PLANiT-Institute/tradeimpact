==Background==

In January 2012, Korea Electric Power Corp (KEPCO) announced an accord with Senegal for a 250 MW coal-fired power station at a cost of US$582.52 million. Under the agreement, KEPCO and the Korean Development Bank would have entirely financed the building of the plant but in return would have had the right to sell its energy output back to Senegal.<ref>[http://leeuniversal.blogspot.com/2012/01/skoreas-kepco-signs-582-mln-senegal.html "S.Korea's KEPCO signs $582 mln Senegal power deal,"] Reuters, January 18, 2012</ref><ref name=tar>[http://www.theafricareport.com/West-Africa/senegals-dakar-out-of-the-dark.html "Senegal's Dakar out of the dark,"] Theafricareport.com, June 29, 2015</ref> Operation was planned for 2017.<ref>[http://www.reuters.com/article/2013/05/16/senegal-kepco-idUSL6N0DX2YD20130516 "South Korea's KEPCO to build coal-fired power plant in Senegal,"] Reuters, May 16, 2013</ref>

After the agreement was signed, KEPCO said that it was abandoning most of its international projects, including the one in Senegal. The government said it was looking for a new partner to pick up where KEPCO left off.<ref name=tar>[http://www.theafricareport.com/West-Africa/senegals-dakar-out-of-the-dark.html "Senegal's Dakar out of the dark,"] Theafricareport.com, June 29, 2015</ref>

An August 2016 report on the 125 MW [[Sendou power station]] said the 250 MW KEPCO project had been called off.<ref>[https://www.afdb.org/fileadmin/uploads/afdb/Documents/Compliance-Review/Management_response_on_the_request_filed_against_the_Sendou_coal_fired_power_plant_project_in_Senegal.pdf "Management Response: Sendou Coal Power Plant Project,"] Africa Development Bank Group, September 26, 2016</ref>
