It is also known as Rugeley-B power station.

==Background==

The power station, also known as Rugeley B, is 1,000 MW and was commissioned in 1972.<ref>Department of Energy and Climate Change, [http://www.decc.gov.uk/media/viewfile.ashx?filetype=4&filepath=Statistics/source/electricity/dukes5_11.xls&minwidth=true "Power stations in the United Kingdom, May 2012"], Department of Energy and Climate Change, May 2012. (This link is to an Excel spreadsheet).</ref> 

Rugeley “A” station, built on the same site in the 1950s, was decommissioned in 1996. Rugeley Power Station is jointly owned by ENGIE (75%) and Mitsui & Co. Ltd. (25%).<ref name=engie>[http://www.rugeleypower.com/?article=19 "Rugeley Power Station expected to cease operations this summer,"] Engie, 8 February 2016</ref>

==Closure==

In February 2016 Engie said the power station is expected to cease market operations in early summer 2016 due to the "deterioration in market conditions for UK coal-fired power generation."<ref name=engie>[http://www.rugeleypower.com/?article=19 "Rugeley Power Station expected to cease operations this summer,"] Engie, 8 February 2016</ref>

A May 2016 Engie press release stated: "Following consultation with employees and unions, ENGIE is now confirming that Rugeley will cease operations by the end of June 2016."<ref>[http://www.rugeleypower.com/?article=21 "RUGELEY POWER STATION - UPDATE,"] Engie, 20 May 2016</ref>

The site of the old coal power station was set to be transformed into a “sustainable village” of 2,000 homes powered by solar panels.<ref>[https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2018/nov/19/rugeley-coal-plant-to-be-transformed-into-a-sustainable-village?utm_source=Greenhouse+Morning+News&utm_campaign=a4388c8f30-Greenhouse_Morning_News_November19th_2018_COPY_01&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_e40c447c1a-a4388c8f30-123999169 "Rugeley coal plant to be transformed into a sustainable village,"] Guardian, November 19, 2018</ref>
