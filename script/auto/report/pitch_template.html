The question

A car sold today is a commitment the destination country has to live with for its whole life.

Trade Impact measures that commitment against the trajectory the destination itself is on. For every vehicle, every year of its operating life: what it emits, minus what the destination's own fleet path implies for the same distance. Positive is emissions added above that path; negative is emissions avoided against it.

Same vehicles, same year, same arithmetic. Only the benchmark changes, and the sign changes with it. That gap is the finding, and everything after this slide is an attempt to say which of the two a decision-maker should act on.

What this does not say

Four limits, stated before anyone else states them.

The limits

  • It is not a company total. of each company's global sales sits inside the assessed markets. Everything here is that slice.
  • The benchmark is allocated, not published. No climate pledge in these markets carries a transport sub-target, so the sector path is taken pro rata from the economy-wide one — which flatters combustion, because transport decarbonises slower than the economy.
  • Vehicle lifetime moves the answer most. Varying it alone takes the committed-path total from to .
  • It is a distance, not a displacement. The number says where a product sits relative to a published path. It is not a claim that the sale caused a change, and it is never netted against a Scope 3 inventory.

What it is for

Three uses it can carry, and one it cannot.

  • Monitoring — the trajectory implied by a named set of sold products, against a named destination path.
  • Targeting — which market and which product class sits furthest from the path.
  • Comparison — the same benchmark applied to every company selling into the same market.
  • Not evaluation — no pre-period, no control group, no causal claim.
