What this is

What does a country's climate path say about the cars it is being sold?

Trade Impact takes one year of a carmaker's sales into one country, and scores every vehicle, every year of its operating life, against the emissions path that country is on. It is a distance to a published trajectory, not a claim about what would have happened otherwise.

TI = what the product emits − what the destination's own path implies for the same distance
    summed over the vehicle's life, in tonnes CO2e.
    positive = emissions added above that path  ·  negative = emissions avoided against it

Two benchmarks are reported for every vehicle and never one alone: S1, the trend the destination's own published series is actually on, and S2, the furthest target its government has committed to. The sign of the answer can change between them, and that is the finding rather than a defect.

Standards

Where this sits between Scope 3 Category 11 and avoided emissions.

Product emissions measured against a destination's own trajectory is neither an inventory line nor an avoided-emissions claim. The register of what has been written about that gap, and where this method differs, is in the reference library.

Trade

What an export portfolio does to an importing country's own commitments.

Border measures price the carbon embodied in making a good. This measures what the good does after it arrives, which no trade instrument currently covers for vehicles.

Not evaluation

The one use this cannot carry.

There is no pre-period, no control group and no counterfactual world here. The number says where a product sits relative to a published path; it is not a claim that the sale caused a change, and it is never netted against a Scope 3 inventory.

What it does not say

Four limits, stated before anyone else states them.

  • It is not a company total.
  • The benchmark is allocated, not published. No government in these markets publishes a car-fleet reduction target, so the sector path is taken pro rata from the economy-wide or whole-transport one. That allocation is ours, not theirs, and it flatters combustion because transport decarbonises more slowly than the economy does.
  • Not every unit carries a result.
  • The inputs are not all of one quality.
